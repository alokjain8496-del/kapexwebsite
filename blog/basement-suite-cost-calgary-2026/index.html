How Much Does a Basement Suite Cost in Calgary? 2026 Guide

A legal basement suite can add flexible living space and rental potential, but the total cost depends heavily on the existing basement and the work required to meet current safety and permit requirements. The most useful starting point is not a one-size-fits-all quote: it is a clear scope based on your property, layout and legalization needs.

What affects the cost of a basement suite?

  • Existing conditions: unfinished basements and older or previously renovated suites can require very different amounts of corrective work.
  • Layout and plumbing: moving drains, adding a kitchen or bathroom, or relocating utilities can materially change the budget.
  • Egress and access: bedroom egress, window wells and entrance requirements may involve structural or excavation work.
  • Fire and life safety: smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms, fire separations and other required upgrades must be planned into the project.
  • Electrical and mechanical work: capacity, heating, ventilation and trade upgrades vary by home.
  • Finishes: kitchens, bathrooms, flooring, cabinetry and sound-control selections can significantly change the final investment.

Typical project budget ranges

For planning purposes, a straightforward basement suite with a favourable existing layout may cost less than a project requiring major plumbing relocation, enlarged windows, structural work or extensive utility upgrades. Calgary permits and fees in 2026

Every secondary suite requires a building permit. The City of Calgary currently lists a total building-permit fee of $403.52 for a new secondary suite and $205.92 to legalize an existing secondary suite. Electrical permits are commonly required, while plumbing, HVAC and development-permit requirements depend on the project and property. Published City timelines are approximate and can change with application completeness and review volumes.

For current rules, fees and timelines, confirm details directly with the City of Calgary before applying. Kapex Infra can help homeowners understand the construction scope and permit path for their specific property.

New suite vs. legalizing an existing suite

Legalizing an existing suite can sometimes reduce demolition and finishing work, but older work may also reveal compliance gaps that need correction. A site review helps identify whether the existing layout, exits, windows, electrical, plumbing and mechanical systems can support legalization or whether a more extensive rebuild is the better option.

Is the $10,000 Calgary secondary-suite incentive available?

The City of Calgary's program can provide up to $10,000 to qualifying homeowners, but funding is limited. As of June 24, 2026, new applications are placed on a waitlist and funding may not be available to everyone who applies. Eligibility and program status should be verified directly with the City before relying on the incentive in your budget.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a basement suite in Calgary?

Yes. Every secondary suite requires a building permit, and additional trade or planning approvals may apply depending on the property and scope.

How long does approval take?

City review timelines vary. For example, Calgary currently lists approximately seven days for a complete existing-suite legalization building-permit review, while a development permit, if required, can take approximately 10 to 12 weeks.

Can an existing basement suite be legalized?

Many existing suites can be assessed for legalization, but the required upgrades depend on the property's current conditions and applicable requirements.
